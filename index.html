The little things
I brought home.

Kyoto Marrakech Lisbon
01

A ritual, not a routine

At a six-seat counter off Teramachi, matcha is whisked with unhurried precision—reminding me how attention turns ordinary minutes into ceremony.

02

A seat at the counter

Warm cedar, hand-thrown stoneware, and a handwritten menu translated with patient smiles over steaming bowls of yuba broth.

03

The long way home

Following the Kamo River after dusk, when paper lanterns reflect in the current and bicycles glide past like quiet whispers.

01

Made slowly, by hand

Inside a sunlit courtyard workshop, artisans chisel glazed terracotta stars one tap at a time—each imperfection proving a human pulse.

02

A place at the table

Freshly baked khobz torn warm beside saffron tagine and crushed green olives, shared on a low brass tray under bitter orange trees.

03

Getting wonderfully lost

Every narrow pink-walled alley opens unexpectedly onto spice pyramids, woven wool, and the distant call echoing across rooftops.

01

A song that stays

Through an open Alfama shutter at midnight, twelve-string Portuguese guitar notes drift down cobbles worn smooth by centuries of footsteps.

02

Still warm from the oven

Flaky pastéis de nata dusted with cinnamon at a corner pastelaria, eaten standing beside locals taking their morning bica.

03

The view from here

From the blue-tiled terrace above the river, terracotta roofs cascade toward the water while gulls wheel in the salt wind.
